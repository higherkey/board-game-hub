# Tabletop Spatial Ergonomics & The Six Zones of Play: Theoretical Reference

> **Document Type:** Pure Theoretical Reference  
> **Source Theory:** Scott Rogers (*Your Turn! The Guide to Great Tabletop Game Design*, Wiley) and Gil Hova (*Ludology* Podcast, Episode 209: "The 6 Zones of Play")

---

## 1. Overview and Core Premise

In physical tabletop game design, player immersion, cognitive load, and interaction friction are fundamentally governed by physical proximity and table ergonomics. 

Game designers **Scott Rogers** and **Gil Hova** formalized this spatial relationship into the **"Six Zones of Play"** framework. The foundational thesis states:

$$\text{Player Attention \& Immersion} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Physical Distance from Player}}$$

When game components, victory conditions, or frequently consulted information are pushed outward away from the player's immediate physical grip and personal workspace, cognitive load rises, physical friction increases, and players suffer from "out-of-sight, out-of-mind" strategic blindness.

---

## 2. The Six Zones of Play

The framework divides the seated player's physical environment into six concentric tiers expanding outward from their body across the table surface:

```
                          [ ZONE 6: Reference / External ]
                                (Rulebooks, Boxes, Web)
                                          ▲
                          [ ZONE 5: Peripheral Sideboards ]
                               (Supply, VP Tracks, Market)
                                          ▲
                          [ ZONE 4: Shared Central Board ]
                               (Communal Arena, Map, Grid)
                                          ▲
                          [ ZONE 3: The Player Tableau ]
                               (Personal Board, Engine, Cards)
                                          ▲
                [ ZONE 2: Non-Dominant Hand ] ── [ ZONE 1: Dominant Hand ]
                      (Staging, Reserves)          (Grip, Active Input)
```

### Zone 1: The Dominant Hand (Immediate Grip & Active Input)
* **Physical Space:** The player's primary hand and the components actively gripped within it.
* **Typical Components:** Cards currently in hand, active dice ready to be rolled, a pen or pencil, a buzzer, tile placement cursor.
* **Ergonomic Role:** High-frequency, immediate physical manipulation.
* **Attention & Immersion:** Highest possible immersion. Represents the player's immediate personal agency and active tactile connection to the game.

### Zone 2: The Non-Dominant Hand (Supporting Hand & Staging)
* **Physical Space:** The secondary hand and its immediate holding zone.
* **Typical Components:** Reserve dice, currency held in reserve, drafted cards waiting to be placed, stabilization of player boards.
* **Ergonomic Role:** Passive holding, staging, and supporting manipulation.
* **Attention & Immersion:** High passive awareness; low immediate cognitive friction.

### Zone 3: The Tableau (Personal Player Workspace)
* **Physical Space:** The table surface directly in front of the seated player, within easy forearm reach (roughly a $12 \times 18\text{ inch}$ boundary).
* **Typical Components:** Personal player board, character sheet, constructed engine of cards/tiles, score tracker, private resource supply.
* **Ergonomic Role:** Personal inventory, engine building, and identity anchor.
* **Attention & Immersion:** Moderate-to-high sustained attention. Semi-public (inspectable by neighboring players, but owned and operated by the individual).

### Zone 4: The Shared Board (Communal Interaction Area)
* **Physical Space:** The geographic center of the table, reachable by all seated players.
* **Typical Components:** The main game board, shared spatial movement grid, communal card river or market, central battle arena.
* **Ergonomic Role:** Communal source of truth; shared strategic context where all players' actions intersect.
* **Attention & Immersion:** High public attention, but physically distant from private manipulation.

### Zone 5: The Sideboard (Auxiliary & Peripheral Systems)
* **Physical Space:** Off-center table extensions, edges of the main board, or separate secondary boards placed beyond easy arm's reach.
* **Typical Components:** General supply bank, discard graveyards, round counters, prestige/fame victory point tracks, modular expansion tiles.
* **Ergonomic Role:** Low-frequency periodic reference, state accounting, and supply management.
* **Attention & Immersion:** Lowest in-game table attention. A primary hazard in game design: if vital scoring rules or player penalties are isolated in Zone 5, players frequently overlook them during active play.

### Zone 6: Reference & External Arbiters
* **Physical Space:** Off-table space, player lap, side chair, or box lid.
* **Typical Components:** Rulebooks, FAQ sheets, scoring pads, companion apps, dice towers located outside the active board.
* **Ergonomic Role:** External arbitration, rule lookup, and game configuration.
* **Attention & Immersion:** Immersion-breaking. Actively pulls player attention out of the "magic circle" of gameplay. High cognitive drag if consulted frequently during active turns.

## 3. Ergonomic Principles & Common Design Anti-Patterns

Tabletop ergonomics identified by Rogers, Hova, and the *Ludology* analyses highlight several recurring spatial failure modes:

| Anti-Pattern | Spatial Mechanism | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Distant Vital Information** | Critical game state (e.g. game-ending trigger) placed in Zone 5 or 6 rather than Zone 1–3. | Players make suboptimal decisions because critical context is out of their natural peripheral vision. |
| **Zone 1 Component Overload** | Forcing players to hold too many distinct components simultaneously (e.g., large card hand + resource tokens + dice). | Physical dropping of pieces, fatigue, awkward fumbling. |
| **Zone 3 / Zone 4 Boundary Blurring** | Unclear distinction between personal cards in play and shared communal cards on the table. | Rules disputes, accidental drafting errors, or misattribution of points. |
| **The Rulebook Crutch** | Requiring repeated mid-turn reference to Zone 6 for iconography or turn sequences. | Severe downtime (AP), disruption of game flow and narrative immersion. |
